    Dark Halos, Loud Binaries
    Primordial black holes through the gravitational-wave lens

    Image: NASA GSFC / J. Schnittman & B. P. Powell

    Hi! I’m Muhsin, a PhD candidate in Applied and Computational Physics at Oakland University, working with Ilias Cholis. I use merger rates to ask whether some of the dark matter is made of black holes that formed in the first moments after the Big Bang.

    My work follows primordial black hole binaries from their birth in the early Universe, through the dark matter halos where they are kicked, perturbed and hardened by close encounters, to the mergers that LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA detects today. Along the way I build merger-rate models across cosmic time, work with collaborators to confront them with the observed black hole catalog, and predict the eccentric signals that LISA and DECIGO could see next. Before my PhD I worked on the other half of the dark sector: dark energy, modified gravity and the Hubble tension.

    More details, with figures from my papers, can be found on my Research page.
